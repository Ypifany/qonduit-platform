Start with what matters

What do you need your home to do for you and the people who live there?

Choose one, combine a few, change the words, or write your own. You are describing why your home matters before we talk about technology.

Qonduit starts the sentence. Change it until it sounds true to you.

Connect technology to purpose

What do you use technology at home to do?

Choose what is actually true in your home. These uses help explain the job your home technology performs.

This should explain how technology contributes to the home statement you just wrote.

Use ordinary names

Who uses the technology, and what technology is there?

Do not include names or private account details. “The box that gives us Wi-Fi” is a useful answer. You can rename it later if you learn the technical term.

Look beyond the walls

What else has to be working for your technology to do what you need?

Your router and devices may be in your home, but they rely on things elsewhere. Choose what your household actually uses. These are outside services and dependencies.

What is a device company account or service?

It is an account, app, or online service run by the company that made a device. The device may use it for setup, updates, storage, remote control, or paid features.

Examples: an Apple Account and iCloud for an iPhone, a Samsung account or SmartThings, a Roku account, Amazon Alexa, Google Home, HP Instant Ink, or a Peloton account. Choose this only when something like this is part of your home system.

Version 1

Make the map tell the truth as you understand it now.

Review the starting map Qonduit made from your answers. Use the controls beside the map only when you want to change what it says.

  1. Add a box: choose “Add something missing,” describe it, then choose “Add to map.”
  2. Show a relationship: choose two boxes and say how one uses, supports, or relies on the other.
  3. Tidy the layout: “Rearrange boxes” only straightens the map. It does not add or change information.

Your map does not need to match anyone else’s. It needs to help you explain what you understand and what you still need to investigate.
